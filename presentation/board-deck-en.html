02 · THE PROBLEM

The problem

A signed contract is not a plan.

Someone still has to read twenty pages of contract prose and rebuild it, by hand, into tasks, dates, owners and acceptance criteria. That takes days of senior time, and every project manager does it differently.

What it costs

Days of expensive time on every engagement, before a single hour of delivery work begins.

What gets lost

The link back to the contract. Six weeks in, nobody can answer “which clause put this on the board?”

What gets hidden

The gaps. The contract never named a launch date, so someone picked one — and it entered the plan looking exactly like a fact.

03 · WHY THIS IS HARD

Why “just use AI” is not the answer

The confident wrong answer is the expensive one.

Any general-purpose AI will produce a polished project plan from any contract in thirty seconds. It will also invent a go-live date, a pricing rate and a named approver — in the same tone of voice as the parts it got right.

There is no way to tell them apart. And a plan whose origins cannot be checked is a plan that cannot be signed off, invoiced against, or defended in a scope dispute.

Drafting the plan is the easy part. Knowing which parts the contract actually authorised is the product.
04 · THE THESIS

The thesis

Every line traces back to the contract.

The document is broken into referenceable passages the moment it arrives. From that point on, every statement the platform produces carries a pointer to the passage that produced it — all the way to the card on the delivery board.

And where the contract is silent, the platform says so. It does not close the gap on the client’s behalf.

06 · KNOWN VS ASSUMED

The distinction that matters

What the contract said, and what we assumed.

Every value in the plan is marked with how it came to exist. This single distinction is what separates the platform from a document that merely sounds convincing.

STATED

The contract says it outright.

Carries a reference to the exact passage.
INFERRED

It follows from something the contract does state.

Carries the passage it follows from.
ASSUMED

The contract is silent. We supplied a working value so planning could continue.

Becomes a recorded assumption and a question for the client.

On the NileBank contract that produced 9 recorded assumptions and 9 client questions — each one a commercial conversation worth having before delivery starts, rather than after.

07 · VERIFICATION

Verification

We check every statement, not the plan as a whole.

A task is not graded once and waved through. It is broken into the individual assertions it makes — this takes fifteen hours, this is the account manager’s responsibility, this cannot start before signature — and each is checked against the contract on its own.

The reason is simple: a task can be entirely right about the work and quietly wrong about the duration. A single overall score hides exactly the detail a project manager needs to catch.

A statement the contract does not support is flagged, not smoothed over.
08 · ACCOUNTABILITY

The control

Nothing reaches the team without a named approval.

The platform never publishes on its own. It prepares the plan, puts every assumption, weak task and unanswered question in front of the project manager, and waits.

Before approval

The manager can edit any task, regenerate a weak one, answer a client question, or reject the plan outright. Every action is recorded.

After approval

Cards are updated rather than duplicated. And if someone changes or deletes a card downstream, the manager is told the plan and the board have diverged.

Accountability

A person signs off. The platform’s job is to make sure that person can see exactly what they are signing.

10 · THE DIFFERENTIATOR

The differentiator

What the platform refuses to do.

  • It refuses a document it cannot genuinely read. A scanned contract stops the process rather than yielding an empty plan that looks complete.
  • It refuses to report success on an empty result. If nothing was extracted, it retries once and then reports failure.
  • It refuses to state a figure it cannot source. An unsupported number in an acceptance criterion is dropped, not saved.
  • It refuses to invent people. Work is assigned to roles; names come only from a real directory.
  • It refuses to fill a silence. Where the contract specifies nothing, the card says so in plain words.

The refusals are the product. Anything can generate a plan. The commercial value is in a system that knows the boundary of what it was told — and stops there.

12 · LIMITS

Where we are honest

Known limits and deliberate trade-offs.

Roles, not named people
Work is assigned to a role, not an individual. There is no HR integration, and inventing staff names is the exact failure this platform exists to prevent. The project manager still makes the final allocation.
The board tool is the weak link
The full plan lives in our own system; the delivery board carries a summary of it. If structured tracking inside the board becomes a requirement, that is the piece to reconsider.
One field is not yet held to the standard
Task priority is currently set without a stated basis and drives nothing. It is the last unsourced value in the system, and it is first on the fix list.
Built for a pilot, not a fleet
One team, no access control, and AI capacity on a trial allowance. All three are known work items before wider rollout.

What comes next

The path to production.

ImmediateProcess long contracts in the background
A large document currently holds up the upload. Moving it to a background job with live progress removes both the wait and the duplicate work it causes.

Staffing runs once. Adding a person today does not fill yesterday’s empty role. It should re-evaluate.

14 · IN CLOSING
In closing
The value is not that it writes the plan. It is that it knows the difference between what the contract said and what it assumed — and tells you which is which.

Every competitor can produce a project plan from a document. What a client is buying here is the ability to open any task on the board, six months into delivery, and read the clause that authorised it.
